# Advisor Comment on `Comprehensive_202608_b.md`

**Date:** August 19, 2026  

## Overall assessment

This version addressed most of the conceptual concerns in my previous comments.

The proposal is therefore much closer to a scientifically defensible comprehensive-exam document.

However, I do **not** yet consider the current version ready for the comprehensive exam.  The main remaining concern is no longer primarily the scientific framing.  It is whether the proposed work can actually be executed at the pace and technical level required for completion of a Ph.D.

The proposal itself now makes this problem visible: most dissertation-level scientific tasks are still listed as not started, while qualification of the in-house ALD system remains incomplete after more than one year of work.

The next revision should therefore concentrate less on adding additional narrative and more on **technical completion, hard decision gates, and accountability for execution**.

---

## 1. Replace the current timeline with hard decision gates

The current timeline gives durations such as “4 months,” “4–6 months,” and an overall estimate of approximately 3–3.5 additional years.  This is not sufficient.

A research plan at this point must contain **calendar-based decision gates**.

For every major gate, provide:

1. an exact calendar deadline;
2. the deliverable that must exist at that deadline;
3. quantitative acceptance criteria;
4. the evidence that will be used to judge the criteria;
5. the action taken if the scientific hypothesis fails;
6. the consequence if the work itself is not completed by the deadline.

The last two items are different and must not be confused.

### Scientific failure

A correctly designed and executed experiment may show that a hypothesis is false.  For example:

- Al₂O₃ may trap nearly all charge;
- no useful near-surface state may exist under the tested LAr conditions;
- retained charge may not transfer efficiently between gates.

These can be legitimate scientific results if the experiment is quantitative, well controlled, reproducible, and provides useful limits.  The appropriate consequence is then scientific redirection.

### Execution failure

Failure to build the required apparatus, complete the measurement, perform the analysis, or produce the required deliverable by an agreed deadline is different.  This is not a negative scientific result.  It is failure to execute the research program.

The proposal must state this distinction clearly.

## 2. The in-house ALD system is the first mandatory gate and a required rite of passage

The proposal correctly states that reactor construction is enabling infrastructure and not the dissertation endpoint.

However, successful construction and qualification of the in-house ALD system has an additional purpose in this Ph.D. program.  It is intentionally a **rite of passage and formal qualifying milestone** for the student.

The purpose is broader than obtaining Al₂O₃ films.  The student is expected to demonstrate the ability to:

- design and assemble a nontrivial experimental system;
- understand and control gas delivery, vacuum, heating, timing, instrumentation, and safety;
- develop and debug the control system;
- diagnose failures systematically;
- design meaningful qualification measurements;
- collect and analyze quantitative data;
- distinguish an apparent effect from a verified result;
- establish repeatable operation;
- characterize the output of the apparatus independently;
- document the system sufficiently that its behavior is understood and reproducible;
- bring the instrument from construction into productive scientific use.

These are core capabilities expected of an experimental Ph.D. student.  The ALD system was selected in part because it provides a contained but technically substantial problem through which these capabilities can be developed and demonstrated.

More than one year has already been spent on this task.  The point of the exercise is therefore not indefinite experience with apparatus construction.  The rite of passage is completed only when the student demonstrates that he can bring the system to a qualified, scientifically useful state.

The revised proposal must give an exact completion date for ALD qualification.

The qualification must include, at minimum:

- why and when shall the activity on TMA/H₂O start;
- successful TMA/H₂O Al₂O₃ deposition on an appropriate smooth substrate;
- demonstrated precursor saturation behavior;
- calibrated growth per cycle;
- measured film thickness;
- thickness repeatability across at least five consecutive runs;
- within-sample uniformity;
- reproducible process operation;
- documented sample handling and contamination control.

Commercial or university-facility Al₂O₃ may be used as a benchmark.  It does not substitute for successful qualification of the in-house system.

The consequence must be stated explicitly:

> **If the in-house ALD system cannot be brought into qualified, reproducible operation by the agreed deadline, this dissertation research program fails.  The result will not be treated as a scientific negative result, reactor construction will not be redefined as the dissertation contribution, and the critical capability will not simply be outsourced in order to bypass this milestone.**

This is intentionally a hard gate.

## 3. Role of the CuOx work

The CuOx work should be understood primarily as a **training, system-development, and capability-building exercise**, rather than as a dissertation science objective.

It has legitimate value.

The CuOx experiments exercise and demonstrate important experimental skills, including:

- controlled gas delivery;
- sequence timing;
- thermal operation;
- hardware/software integration;
- automated data logging;
- run-to-run reproducibility analysis;
- handling of real experimental variability;
- XRD and Raman characterization;
- comparison against literature;
- interpretation of incomplete or conflicting measurements;
- development of disciplined experimental judgment.

For this reason, the CuOx work should not simply be dismissed as irrelevant.  It is part of the process by which the student is expected to develop the experimental capability required for independent Ph.D.-level research.

However, its role must be stated correctly.

CuOx is **not** a scientific target of this dissertation.  It does not answer the liquid-noble-gas interface question, and it does not by itself qualify the ALD system for the intended research.

The appropriate interpretation is:

> The CuOx experiments are a system-development and training stage used to establish experimental competence and exercise the apparatus under real process conditions.  Their success is useful evidence of developing capability, but they are not the dissertation scientific result and do not satisfy the ALD qualification milestone.

The acquired capability must now be translated into:

1. reproducible TMA/H₂O Al₂O₃ growth;
2. quantitative ALD qualification;
3. controlled variation of Al₂O₃ surface properties;
4. eventual liquid-noble-gas interface measurements.

Detailed Cu₂O/CuO phase analysis may remain in the Appendix as evidence of the work performed and the student's characterization ability.  The main text should emphasize the conclusions relevant to system readiness and the skills demonstrated.

The key question is no longer whether the student can continue learning from CuOx.  It is whether the student can now use the capabilities developed during that work to complete the intended ALD system and move into the dissertation science.

## 4. Subsequent gates must also have real consequences

The remaining stages should use the same structure.

### Electrostatic, source, and readout readiness gate

Before fabrication or LAr interface measurements proceed, there must be:

- a specified device geometry;
- calculated electric-field and electron-potential-energy maps;
- a stated vertical holding or reversible-localization mechanism;
- a concrete electron source design;
- estimated charge per injection;
- an equivalent circuit;
- a line-item noise budget;
- calculated signal size and signal-to-noise ratio;
- identified amplifier, capacitances, bandwidth, and digitization chain.

These should be completed calculations and designs, not statements that they will be calculated later.

If this gate is not completed by its deadline, device fabrication and cryogenic experimentation should not proceed.  Continued inability to complete the design work should trigger a formal assessment of whether the dissertation plan remains executable.

### Stage 1: retention and recovery gate

Failure of the **hypothesis** is acceptable if a well-designed experiment establishes a quantitative upper bound on recoverable charge or retention time.

Failure to construct and operate the experiment, obtain calibrated data, or reach an interpretable result by the agreed deadline is not equivalent.  That should trigger a formal progress review and possible termination of the present Ph.D. research path.

### Stage 2 and later gates

The same distinction should continue:

- failure of a physical mechanism → analyze, publish or document the result, and redirect;
- failure to execute the agreed experiment → progress failure requiring formal review.

The proposal should not assume that every physical stage must succeed.  It should require that every stage be **decisively resolved** within a defined period.

## 5. Reconsider the numerical stage gates

I support the use of quantitative stage gates, but several current values appear to be planning numbers rather than physically justified thresholds.

Examples include:

- 10% recoverable fraction;
- 1 ms retention;
- 50% two-region transfer;
- 90% per-step multi-gate transfer;
- 5% charge loss per repeated-sensing cycle.

These may be acceptable as provisional engineering thresholds, but the proposal should explain why each value represents a useful decision boundary.

In particular, a 5% loss on every repeated-sensing cycle is not close to what would ultimately be meant by Skipper-like non-destructive sensing.  It may be sufficient to demonstrate reversible shuttling in an early experiment, but the proposal should distinguish:

1. initial demonstration of repeated movement and remeasurement; and
2. eventual genuinely non-destructive repeated sensing with sufficiently small loss to provide useful noise reduction.

Similarly, averaging measurements from many independently injected packets can improve the precision of a measured transfer efficiency, but this is not the same as repeatedly measuring the same packet.

Keep these concepts separate.

## 6. Resolve internal contradictions

There are several places where the prose and tables do not agree.

For example, the main text correctly says that an arbitrary fixed-charge-density cutoff should not be imposed and that the measured fixed charge should instead be included in the electrostatic model.

The Appendix then imposes a surface-entry criterion of approximately $10^{12}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}$.

I prefer the first formulation.

Measure the actual fixed charge, include it in the model, and determine whether the resulting field landscape permits the required device operation.  Remove an arbitrary universal cutoff unless a calculation demonstrates why that cutoff is physically required.

Other proposed numerical criteria should receive the same treatment.
